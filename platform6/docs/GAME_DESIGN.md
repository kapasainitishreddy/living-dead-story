# STILL HERE — A Living Dead Story

## High concept

**STILL HERE** is a cinematic mystery adventure about a city that has forgotten why it wants tomorrow.

At exactly 3:17 AM, people did not die. They kept eating, commuting, working, and speaking, but desire itself began disappearing. When someone loses the last thing connecting them to life, pieces of the city begin to vanish. The phenomenon is known as **The Living Dead**.

The player can see remnants of things people once cared about. These remnants are called **Threads**. By solving mysteries, reconstructing memories, helping people, and occasionally choosing tiny real-world actions, the player restores the city while gradually discovering what matters to them.

This is a game first. It must never feel like therapy homework wearing game graphics.

## Product promise

> A city has forgotten why it wants tomorrow. Find out why.

The player returns because they want to solve the mystery and see what happens next. The potentially helpful mechanisms sit underneath the narrative: agency, curiosity, competence, connection, achievable action, reflection, and personally chosen meaning.

## Tone

- haunting but warm
- mysterious rather than preachy
- emotionally mature
- sometimes funny and absurd
- hopeful without forced positivity
- no guilt for absence
- no streak anxiety
- no fake therapist voice

## Opening

Black screen. Refrigerator hum. Rain against glass.

The protagonist wakes on a couch at **3:17 AM**.

The city outside is intact but almost completely dark. Trains still move in the distance. Thousands of windows are black.

Then one window across the city turns warm yellow.

The first objective appears:

> **FIND THE LIGHT**

## Chapter 1 vertical slice

1. Apartment at 3:17 AM
2. One glowing window appears
3. Dead radio activates
4. Message: `PLATFORM 6 DOES NOT EXIST`
5. Message repeats: `DO NOT BOARD THE 3:17 TRAIN`
6. Walk through the rainy Quiet City
7. Enter Central Station
8. Discover hidden Platform 6
9. Meet the silent conductor / Elias thread
10. Investigate a glowing ticket and missing photograph
11. Complete an environmental puzzle
12. The train cannot move until "something on your side moves"
13. Player chooses an optional tiny real-world action or skips it
14. A successful action causes an enormous fantasy-world response: sequential carriage lights, sound design, rain/reflection shift, movement
15. First Thread is discovered
16. Conductor reveal: "The dead aren't losing their reasons. Someone is taking them."
17. Cliffhanger

The first polished chapter should be 20–30 minutes and strong enough that playtesters immediately ask for Chapter 2.

## World districts

### The Quiet City
Rain, dark towers, wet streets, distant trains, warm isolated windows. The visual baseline.

### Central Station / Platform 6
Impossible architecture, destination boards reading UNKNOWN, trains that react to player history.

### The Observatory — Wonder
Above the clouds. Unanswered questions become stars in a personal constellation.

### The Greenhouse — Care / Patience
An abandoned botanical station slowly reclaimed by life. Growth happens over time, not through grinding.

### The Workshop — Creation
Huge unfinished machines, sketches, boats built before anyone knows an ocean exists.

### The Diner — Connection
Recurring human stories. The player cannot fix everyone. Sometimes being present is the only action.

### The Arcade — Joy
Ridiculous minigames and playful color. The game explicitly validates small, unserious pleasures.

### The Memory District
Partially flooded architecture. Memories appear as reflections beneath black water. Reconstruction puzzles live here.

### The Shore — Letting go / Experience
Discovered much later. The city was never the whole world.

### The Central Door
Visible from early in the game. Expected to hide a final boss. When it finally opens, the player sees daylight for the first time and discovers a larger world.

## Antagonist: The Archivist

The Archivist is not a cartoon depression monster. Its philosophy is coherent and tempting:

- wanting love creates grief
- wanting success creates disappointment
- wanting tomorrow creates fear
- caring makes people vulnerable
- therefore, remove wanting

The Archivist believes it is protecting people from suffering by severing their Threads.

The player cannot defeat it through conventional violence. The game itself becomes the rebuttal: meaning can create pain, but a life without caring is not the same as peace.

## Core progression: Threads

Never show a simplistic "happiness score."

Possible Thread families:

- Wonder — curiosity, questions, exploration
- Bond — people, animals, relationships
- Making — creating, writing, building, art
- Becoming — identity and growth
- Experience — places, sensations, events
- Duty — things or people depending on the player
- Defiance — refusal to let this chapter be the ending
- Mastery — learning and competence
- Care — nurturing self, others, or living things
- Rest — safety, quiet, restoration

The system should infer soft preferences from repeated explicit choices, never diagnose the player or announce "your purpose is X."

## Core gameplay loop

Mystery
→ Explore
→ Environmental puzzle / investigation
→ NPC choice
→ Optional tiny real-world action
→ Large in-game consequence
→ Discover a Thread / clue
→ Permanent city transformation
→ Cliffhanger or unresolved question
→ Return when wanted

## Session modes

### I WANT TO PLAY
Full story, exploration, puzzles, 10–30 minutes.

### GIVE ME SOMETHING SMALL
Night Run, 2–5 minutes. One objective, one beautiful payoff.

### JUST STAY WITH ME
No objectives. Quiet space, Sanctuary, ambient exploration, optional contact/support tools.

## Real-world actions

These should be tiny, optional, privacy-preserving, and never require proof.

Examples:

- drink water
- open a curtain
- look outside for ten seconds
- walk to another room and back
- stretch
- step outside briefly
- send a low-pressure message or meme
- make something warm
- put one object back where it belongs
- notice one far-away object
- write one unfinished thought

Every action flow must include **Skip / Not tonight** with no punishment.

The fantasy consequence should be disproportionately satisfying: a train powers up, an entire district receives electricity, stars appear, water starts moving, music gains a new instrument.

## No-punishment design rules

- no streak loss
- no companion sadness because the player did not return
- no daily guilt notifications
- no FOMO tied to vulnerable states
- no crisis achievements
- no proof-of-care surveillance
- no paywall for safety/support features
- no loss of world progress after absence

If the player returns after months, Ember simply wakes and welcomes them back.

## Ember

A tiny spark-like companion representing the part of the world/player that is still responsive.

Ember evolves aesthetically and behaviorally but never needs the player to keep it alive. It sleeps when the player is gone.

Return line example:

> "There you are."

## Long-term systems

### Sanctuary
Private, local-first space for:
- Threads
- Future Echo letters
- notes
- meaningful images
- optional voice snippets
- unfinished stories
- constellation
- trusted contacts
- personal safety-plan data

Sensitive content should be encrypted/local-first wherever practical.

### Future Echoes
On stronger days, the player can leave a message for a future version of themselves. The game returns it later in an appropriate context. These are the player's own words, not generated motivational copy.

### Unfinished Stories
Never call them a productivity to-do list. They are things the player may want to continue: learn guitar, finish an app, see an ocean, call someone, watch a film, visit somewhere, create something.

### Story Director
Selects authored content based on:
- requested session length
- explicit energy preference
- unlocked locations
- unresolved NPC arcs
- previous choices
- soft Thread tendencies
- puzzle difficulty history

AI may orchestrate authored material or fill low-stakes connective tissue. AI must not act as a therapist, diagnose the player, generate crisis counseling, or invent high-risk clinical guidance.

## Infinite structure

Finite authored main mystery + effectively infinite living world.

After the main story:
- recurring NPC arcs continue
- Night Runs continue
- districts change over time
- player-created unfinished stories continue
- procedural environmental variations continue
- authored story archetypes can be recombined

Do not rely on unlimited LLM-generated emotional stories. Hand-author the emotional spine and use systems to vary context, order, location, clues, and consequences.

## Visual direction

Target feeling: premium indie cinematic experience, not wellness-app UI.

Visual arc:

**death → flicker → life**

Start with:
- muted palette
- rain
- fog
- low wind
- sparse music
- dark towers
- isolated bloom
- subtle film grain
- reflective roads

As the world restores:
- windows illuminate
- vegetation moves
- water gains reflections
- particles appear
- NPC silhouettes return
- weather gains warmth
- color subtly expands
- soundtrack gains instruments

Major visual moments should be authored and memorable.

## Technology direction

Primary production engine: **Godot 4**.

Suggested supporting tools:
- Blender for original/modified 3D assets
- Godot shaders for rain, fog, water, bloom-like effects, color restoration
- open assets during prototyping from sources such as Poly Haven, ambientCG, Kenney, and Quaternius after verifying the license for every imported asset
- web prototype remains useful for low-friction playtesting and marketing demos

Prioritize mobile performance:
- Android first
- iOS second
- PC build as useful for testing / potential Steam release
- scalable quality tiers
- baked lighting where appropriate
- optimized particles
- occlusion/culling
- reduced-motion option

## Safety architecture

The game is not emergency care and must not pretend to be clinically proven.

If the player explicitly indicates immediate danger:
- exit fictional progression
- remove scores/rewards
- show a clear safety/support screen
- allow trusted contacts / safety plan
- localize crisis resources by country for production
- never let an LLM freestyle crisis instructions

Before marketing specifically to vulnerable populations, obtain review from:
- licensed clinician with suicide-prevention experience
- lived-experience advisors
- game UX researcher
- privacy/security reviewer

## What success means

Do not optimize primarily for time spent.

Measure:
- did players want another chapter?
- where did they become bored?
- did they understand the objective?
- did optional real-world actions feel useful, neutral, annoying, or patronizing?
- did the world feel responsive to their choices?
- voluntary return without guilt systems
- narrative attachment to NPCs
- completion of meaningful story beats
- emotional-safety feedback

The first major test is simple:

> Do players finish Chapter 1 and immediately want to know what is behind the next signal?
